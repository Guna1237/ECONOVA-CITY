# ECONOVA: CITY: player quick start

This is a plain-language handout for players, based on `GAME_DESIGN_SPEC.md`. It explains the approved rules in friendly terms. It does not replace them, and if anything here ever seems to disagree with the spec, the spec wins.

## Send before the game

Welcome to ECONOVA: CITY. Your group of 4 to 6 players competes to build the strongest property portfolio over 8 rounds. Whoever has the highest score when the game ends wins.

Here is the shape of it: you buy properties, develop them, collect income from them, and use cards and Influence to steer the city in your favor. Your score at the end is not just the Credits sitting in your account. It also counts your properties, any districts you control, whatever Influence you have left, and your secret objective if you complete it.

Before you sit down to play, bring a charged phone. Your organizer will send you a player link. Open it, enter your room code and your name, and keep that browser tab open for the whole game. Each room is its own separate game, completely independent from any other room running at the same time. Everything you need is on your phone: the board, your cards and decisions, and City updates for news, policies, rent and trade results. No projector is needed.

Tap **How to play** for a short reminder, or tap any board space to learn what happens there. **Your next step** takes you to your controls. For quiet cues, tap **Sound off** to turn sound on. Sound is optional and starts muted when you open the game.

You start the game with 1,000 Credits, 5 Influence, and 2 Strategy Cards. You will also be shown two secret objectives and asked to pick one. Keep your choice to yourself. Nobody else needs to know, and if you complete it by the end of the game, it earns you 200 extra points.

## Keep nearby while playing

### On your turn

1. Roll when the game prompts you. The server moves your piece forward and applies whatever effects come from that movement. The only exception is if you hold the Shortcut card and have not played a card yet this turn: then the game asks whether you want to spend it to move backward instead. Without that card you always move forward, and you will not be asked.
2. Resolve the space you land on. Depending on where you land, you might be offered a property to buy, owe a landing fee, draw a card, or face an event choice.
3. Use your available Actions, then end your turn. You normally get 2 Actions per turn. Keep an eye on how many you have left, since some card effects can use one up earlier in the turn than you'd expect.

Buying the property you land on, and starting its auction if you decline, do not use up one of your 2 Actions. Those are free.

### What can I do with an Action?

- **Develop.** Improve a property you already own, up to Level 3. You do not need to be standing on it to develop it, but you cannot develop a property you only just bought this same turn.
- **Use Influence.** Spend 1 Influence to raise or lower one district's Demand by 1, when the game allows it. Demand affects how much income a property earns and how much a landing fee costs.
- **Trade.** Propose swapping Credits or properties with another player. They have to agree to it before anything changes hands.
- **Play a Strategy Card.** Follow the timing and cost printed on the card itself. You can play at most one card per turn, though some cards are played outside the normal Action phase entirely.

### Properties and Credits

Properties earn you income at the end of every round. When another player lands on a property you own, they may owe you a fee. Developing a property raises its income, its landing fee, and its final value at the end of the game. Events and city policies can also change costs and returns along the way, so keep an eye on what is currently in effect.

If you move forward across or onto City Center, you earn 150 Credits, at most once per turn. Moving backward, for example with a Shortcut card, does not trigger that bonus.

### The four special spaces

Four spaces on the board are not properties: City Center, Innovation Hub, Market Square, and Observatory.

City Center is the only safe one. Landing there or passing over it just earns you the 150 Credits, and nothing else happens.

Innovation Hub, Market Square, and Observatory all work exactly the same way as each other. Landing on any one of them draws one random city event from a shared pool of eight. You cannot tell in advance which of the three you will get, and none of them favors one particular outcome over another. The eight possible results are:

- Gain 80 Credits.
- Gain 2 Influence.
- Choose a district to raise its Demand by 1.
- Choose a district to lower its Demand by 1.
- Draw one Strategy Card.
- Your next development this turn costs 30 Credits less, to a minimum of 10.
- Gain 1 Influence and 40 Credits.
- Whichever district currently has the highest Demand earns 10 extra income this round, for every property in it.

So Innovation Hub can just as easily hand you a Demand shift or a discount as a card, and Market Square or Observatory can just as easily hand you a card. Nothing about the space itself decides the outcome, only landing on one of the three does.

### Auctions and Council votes

If you decline a property, it goes to auction. An auction lasts up to 30 seconds. You submit one sealed bid or pass. The highest bid wins, and if two players tie, the win goes to whoever comes first in the current round's turn order. If everyone bids or passes before the clock runs out, the auction finishes early.

At the start of Rounds 3 and 6, the City Council offers two policies for the table to decide on. You get 45 seconds to choose how much of your Influence to put behind each option, or to abstain entirely. You do not have to spend all of it. If the vote ties, Option A wins. Your own allocation stays private the whole time, even after the result is announced. Only the winning policy and the total votes are shown publicly.

### Watch the timer

A normal turn lasts 45 seconds, with a warning after 30 seconds (15 seconds left). Auctions and emergency property sales each run on their own separate 30 second timer. While one of those is happening, your regular turn timer pauses, then picks back up afterward. Council votes have their own timer too. Reading instructions does not pause the game for anyone, but if an operator pauses the game, every timer freezes until they resume it.

Two decisions happen outside a normal turn, and each gives you 45 seconds. First, choose your secret objective at the start. In Round 4, if your hand is full, choose a card to discard. If the timer runs out, the game keeps your first offered objective or discards your lowest numbered card. Choose before the deadline to keep control of that decision.

### If you cannot pay a landing fee

If a landing fee comes due and you do not have enough Credits, you get 30 seconds to choose properties for an emergency sale. Each one sells for half its current value, rounded down. If the timer runs out, or if you disconnect, the server will sell properties for you automatically, starting with the lowest liquidation value and breaking ties by the lowest property ID. If that still is not enough to cover the fee, you pay whatever Credits you have left and stay in the game either way. You cannot trigger an emergency sale just to free up spending money when you do not actually owe a fee.

If you hold an Insurance Policy card, you can play it right before paying a landing fee to reduce that fee to zero. Playing it uses 1 Action from your current turn, and it counts against your one-card-per-turn limit like any other card.

### If the connection drops

Stay on the same tab and let it reconnect on its own. Try not to tap the same action over and over while you wait. If the game tells you an action's result is uncertain, take a moment to check the refreshed board and your resources before you try anything else. Do not join again as if you were a new player, that will not recover your seat. If your connection or your seat truly will not come back, ask your organizer for help.

### How do I win?

After Round 8 ends, your score adds up:

- Whatever Credits you have left.
- The final value of everything you own.
- Any bonuses for controlling a full district.
- 10 points for every point of Influence you still have.
- 200 points if you completed your secret objective.

Whoever has the highest total wins. If two or more players are tied, it is broken first by remaining Credits, then by property value, then by number of properties owned. If all three of those are still tied, the players share the win.

## Organizer's 30-second introduction

"Your goal is the highest score after eight rounds. Buy properties, improve them, and work toward your secret objective. On your turn, roll, follow the prompt, then use your available Actions and end your turn. Watch your phone between turns too: auctions, trades and Council votes may need you. Open City updates for news, policies and receipts. Keep your game tab open, and tell me if your connection does not recover."
